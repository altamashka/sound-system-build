# Our first speaker

We’re starting with **one JMOD**: a speaker for the middle and higher parts of the music. Once it’s built, we’ll review the cost and work before deciding whether to build **one SKRAM** bass speaker.

## What we do next

1. **Name two people:** one to organise the shopping and costs, and one to check the cutting files. They can bring others in to help.
2. **Agree a first-build budget and ownership.** Decide how much each person can contribute, who holds the money, and what happens if someone leaves. We don’t have a reliable total yet.
3. **Get the designer’s files.** The files aren’t in this GitHub copy. The person checking them needs the original JMOD package and Fusion design software.
4. **Check the workshop and cutting plan.** Confirm access and training, machine and storage costs, available tools, sheet capacity, and space for assembly. Check whether the supplied cutting layout can produce one cabinet and how much wood it needs. The design includes curved cuts and sheets that must be turned over.
5. **Price the list below.** Check current prices, delivery and availability. Add workshop fees and a little for small supplies; bring the total back to the group before ordering.
6. **Test the cutting setup.** The person preparing the files must check the machine settings, sheet thickness, tools and how each sheet is held and lined up after turning. Run the software’s cutting simulation and make a test cut before cutting the full sheets. The earlier file review did not simulate or cut the job. See [Build Guide B5](Build%20Guide.md#b5-cam-review).

After that, buy the agreed materials, cut the parts and follow [Build Guide B](Build%20Guide.md#b--jmod-build) to assemble the cabinet. Keep a record of what we spend and how long it takes.

## First JMOD: shopping list

This is a **list to price**, not an order. Check the full list and quantities in [Build Guide B3](Build%20Guide.md#b3-bill-of-materials): its table is for **two cabinets**.

| Item | For one JMOD | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Birch plywood | 12 mm and 18 mm, uncoated | Quantity depends on the checked cutting layout. Confirm birch throughout, glue, availability and VAT. |
| B&C 12NDL88 speaker units | 2 | The previous plan used 8-ohm versions. Check compatibility with the amplifier plan. |
| B&C DCX464 speaker unit | 1 | The previous plan used the 16-ohm version. |
| B&C FB464 crossover | 1 | Match it to the 16-ohm DCX464; the crossover divides sound between parts of the speaker. Confirm the version and amplifier plan. |
| Handles and gaskets | 2 of each | Check the dimensions against the design. |
| Screws, fittings, connectors and internal wire | See Build Guide B3 | Price the quantities for one cabinet, including a few spares. |
| Glue, filler, gasket tape, finish and printing material | As needed | Check what the workshop already supplies. The adapter inside the cabinet must be 3D printed. |

**Wood price reference:** on 23 October, the Alt Makers member list showed €139.83 per 12 mm sheet and €167.79 per 18 mm sheet, with VAT unconfirmed. The supplied layout for **two** JMODs uses two 12 mm sheets and one 18 mm sheet: €447.45 at those displayed prices. That is not a quote or a confirmed price for one cabinet. Check the layout and current prices before buying. [Wood sourcing review](Wood%20sourcing%20review.md)

The speaker units, crossover, hardware, finish, printing, workshop fees and delivery still need current prices. Don’t use the wood figure as the total build budget.

## After the first JMOD

Decide together whether to build a second JMOD or one SKRAM next. The previous SKRAM plan used one B&C 21DS115 speaker unit and four sheets of birch plywood per cabinet; check the amplifier match and cutting layout before buying. A finished SKRAM is estimated to weigh about 70 kg, so plan lifting, transport and storage. Details: [Build Guide C](Build%20Guide.md#c--skram-build).

Before playing either speaker, choose suitable amplifiers and a sound processor, then have an experienced person check the wiring and protective settings. The processor divides the sound and helps protect the speaker units. Follow the [Sound Setup Guide](Sound%20Setup%20Guide.md) and equipment manuals; a calculated setting alone is not proof that it is safe.
